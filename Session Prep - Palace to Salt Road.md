# From the Palace to the Salt Road

*GM notes for this week. The four entries below are situations you can bring into play in whichever order fits the table.*

**Starting position.** The rulers survived the attic ambush. They have met Don Beltrano, who agreed to fence palace treasure if they kill the Velascan assassin. Voros is waiting for the grain audience. Rodorigo is moving on San Severo with two hundred troops. The old session log already describes the column on the road. Use this week's outline for the order of play and keep the exact departure time flexible.

**Have ready.** A palace key register, a copied collection notice, a grain manifest, and a death warrant. One line on a scrap of paper is enough for each. The warrant can stay folded until the road.

## Judith’s empty room

**Open with Fernazo.** He has Judith's name in the household register and two servants' accounts that disagree about which attic doors were regularly opened. Her duties have been reassigned. The kitchen staff now go upstairs in pairs.

> “Judith has gone, Your Majesties. The register and the servants tell me different things about who had keys to that attic. I’d rather ask them here than have the General ask them downstairs.”

**People who can talk.** Fernazo wants a quiet accounting of the keys and a household he can still run. Ignako Morasto is separately collecting accounts of footsteps in the disused galleries. A kitchen servant knows Judith’s ordinary routine and can describe when she was last seen working. If you need a physical detail, her work apron still hangs on its peg.

**GM knowledge.** Judith led the rulers to the hidden trove, left, and the undead beast ambushed them. She belongs to the Republican Insurgents. Her current location, who supplied the beast, and whether the White Rose authorized the attack are open questions. Let the household inquiry establish her absence immediately and produce names and testimony about access. An inconsistent register can support several explanations.

## Savarro and the dockside murders

**Beltrano's position.** The agreement stands. He is changing collection signals and keeping his buyers out of sight. Joachor can get word to the wharf. Savarro “The Caulker” Vezan wants to break the Night Guild’s fencing network so the Velasco Syndicate can take over its trade.

**How Savarro picks his victims.** A thief can steal silver, but a fence knows who will buy it, where to hand it over, and how to signal that the exchange is safe. Oren knew the buyers. Marta knew how to move the goods. Belos kept watch. With those people gone, the Syndicate can offer thieves a way to sell again on Velascan terms. Beltrano would have to expose his remaining buyers to move palace treasure while Savarro is still using Oren’s book.

| Where the investigation lands | What is there |
| --- | --- |
| Charnel Wharf | Savarro posed as a sailor selling damaged silver, killed Oren the Weigher, and took his buyer book. He left the purse. A surviving porter remembers the supposed sailor asking about water depth. A replacement lookout is now on duty. |
| Surviving fences | A false collection notice repeats Oren’s old abbreviation for the west quay. Savarro drew Marta Reed to a tidal stair using a signal from the book. Her empty eel-traps washed up later. |
| Belos’s counting room | Savarro left Belos’s rooftop warning lamp burning after killing him, so the guild would think it still had eyes on the wharf. The wick is fresh shipwright’s tow. |
| Salt-Fish Smokehouse | Savarro keeps copied signals, key impressions, and payment records disguised as repair invoices. A hired skiff carries papers between the smokehouse and the grounded hulks where he changes berths. |

**The false collection notice.** A fence gets a discreet slip saying where and when to pick up stolen goods. It looks like an ordinary delivery. A familiar phrase or mark tells the fence it came from someone they trust. Use “W. Qy.” for Oren’s old shorthand and add a little hook beside the second bell as the retired signal.

> Two baskets. W. Qy. After the second bell.

**Put a lead in motion.** A surviving fence brings Joachor a fresh notice because it uses a signal Beltrano just retired. If you need a visible next lead, the porter recognizes the skiff from runs to the smokehouse. For a sighting of Savarro, give him tar in his fingernails, a pale rope burn on one wrist, and a roll of shipwright’s tools. He is watching the replacement lookout and mapping the ferrymen who approach Beltrano’s cistern. Inés wants Oren’s original book, and Savarro is holding back a copy as insurance. If his notices stop working, he can shift to watching the ferrymen in person.

## Voros at the High Alcázar

**The offer on the table.** Commodore Nikolos Voros arrives with Factor Demetrio Phokas and four marines. The manifest lists 50,000 bushels offshore. The public asking price is 45,000 gold sovereigns. Voros wants payment his consortium will honor and a safe return to his fleet. Demetrio wants the manifests left unaudited. Play Voros as direct and watchful. He taps his seal ring on the table when talk drifts from payment.

> “My factor has brought the manifest. Your chancellor has declined the price. Tell me what I’m to take back to my ships.”

**What puts pressure on the audience.** Voros has pledged his family estate against his stake in the cargo, and the lower holds are beginning to spoil. Tiberio wants delay until Kordava’s hunger forces a concession over the Baracha Strait. Demetrio has an older shortage of 2,000 bushels to conceal. If quantities come up, have Demetrio distinguish the manifest from a fresh tally at unloading. If the audience stalls, Voros can have the harbor pilot calculate the next usable tide while Tiberio presses his treaty. For an improvised compromise, Voros can discuss a small paid delivery without promising to release the fleet’s cargo.

**If the audience runs late.** Korinna has standing orders to take the fleet out and fire on the lower timber wharves if Voros is imprisoned or fails to return by the third night bell. Voros can ask twice what bell it is and send a marine to check his boat, so his concern is visible before the situation comes to a head.

### Optional encounter at the harbor boom

While Voros is at the palace, Demetrio sends a few sacks ashore for inspection on a lighter, a small cargo boat. Two Velascan skiffs come out from under the wharf. One grapples the lighter and starts passing sacks over. The other tries to tow it into a side channel. The pilot cuts the towline, but the tide swings the loaded boat broadside against the harbor boom. The deck tilts, sacks slide, and an Argossean patrol boat rows in with crossbows ready.

- **The Velascan crews want the grain.** They plan to sell it cheaply in the Lower Wards and take credit for feeding people. The skiff alongside has to decide how many sacks it can load before it casts off.
- **The pilot wants his crew clear.** A deckhand is hanging over the side as the tide presses the boat into the boom. The pilot shouts for a line from the wharf.
- **The Argossean patrol wants its cargo back.** From the water, the marines have a poor view of who boarded first. They call for the boats to hold fast as they close.

At the audience, a harbor watch messenger brings word just as Voros is asking who guarantees safe passage for his cargo. At the docks, start with the boom chain groaning and the deckhand losing his grip. The tide keeps changing the gaps between boats and the wharf. When the lighter comes free or the skiffs scatter, note who ended up with the sacks and what the patrol reports. That report gives Voros a concrete reason to revisit safe passage at the bargaining table.

## The road to San Severo

**Show what the troops are marching into.** On the Salt Highway, a salt wagon comes back empty. Its driver says the villagers have barricaded the only bridge that takes heavy wagons. Crown bailiffs have been driven out, and the village is refusing grain requisitions while it shelters refugees. The driver mostly wants to know whether the army will take his horses. At the next halt, an orderly brings Rodorigo three warrants naming Androvo, Madre Soledor, and Alcalde Kadenzo. The general checks Androvo’s name first.

> “The bridge carries every heavy wagon on this road. I need it open. Androvo knows what an army can do with a crossing.”

### Optional skirmish at the lime cut

At a bend where the Salt Highway cuts through a white gypsum bank, a riderless army horse comes back dragging a stirrup. Ahead, five of Rodorigo’s outriders are dismounting behind a low roadside wall. Seven of Androvo’s people have pulled an army courier off his horse and are taking him through a dry wash toward San Severo. The bend hides the fight from the main column. The first infantry can reach the wall before the wagons can get around it.

- **Androvo’s patrol wants the courier alive.** They want to learn when the bridge assault is coming. Two cover the wash from the bank while the others withdraw with the prisoner. Their leader offers to stop shooting if the outriders give them room to leave.
- **The outrider sergeant wants the satchel back.** The courier dropped it beside a roadside shrine when he fell. It contains marching orders for the dawn assault and a separate sealed instruction to muster the hound-masters at the chapel. The sergeant sends two riders toward the shrine under cover of crossbow fire.
- **Rodorigo wants his papers.** When the column catches up, he asks about the satchel before asking who was taken. He can send infantry up the bank or call the outriders back to keep his march moving.

Start with a bolt striking the shrine and the courier shouting that he can walk if they loosen his wrists. The wall gives cover, the bank gives a clear view, and the wash gives the patrol a way out. End the skirmish once the patrol gets clear, gives up the courier, or loses its way out. The courier knows the attack is planned for dawn. The papers can reveal the chapel detail early. The original Dispatch of the Black River is still hidden in the altar.

### The Dispatch of the Black River

During the Restoration Civil War, Rodorigo sent Androvo a sealed order to murder thirty unarmed Church of Nemeth emissaries carrying peace terms, burn the bodies in a lime pit, and plant forged republican pamphlets to justify continuing the war. Androvo refused, broke his sword, and deserted with the original. It is now in a lead case inside the chapel altar.

The dispatch shows what Rodorigo ordered. Androvo can testify that he received the order and refused it. This gives Rodorigo a private reason to move quickly against the rebel commander and send trusted men to the chapel while the rest of the army concentrates on the bridge.

| Person | What they’re doing |
| --- | --- |
| Rodorigo | Prepares a dawn assault on the bridge and summary executions. His trusted men have orders to burn the chapel during the attack. |
| Androvo de Ojedo | Holds the crossing with the Brotherhood of Thorns. He kept the Dispatch of the Black River, which orders the murder of thirty church peace emissaries in Period VII. The original is in a lead case in the chapel altar. |
| Madre Soledor | Shelters refugees in the chapel. Her appeal to Velasco has reached an armed relief force coming upriver. The river determines when it arrives. |
| Alcalde Kadenzo | Is detained in town and named on a royal warrant. He is looking for protection and trying to keep his hidden customs money secret. |

**If the table stays on the road,** end at the first sight of the basalt bridge. A sentry counts the barricades aloud while one of Rodorigo’s officers asks where the hound-masters are to muster. If they reach the town, the bridge, chapel, and granary give you three immediate places with people doing things. Keep the relief force and dawn preparations in mind as events in the world.
